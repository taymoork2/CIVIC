# A Laundered Closet

## Product context

A household laundry-care app: record clothes and care labels, inspect products, plan a wash, confirm at the machine, prepare, wash, dry, and put away. Home, Closet, Laundry, Household, and Me are the five root destinations. Home is state-led; task screens are linear, record-led, and free of decorative heroes.

Historical Claude Design implementation evidence is retained under [source_examples/](source_examples/README.md). This package extends the evidence into reusable tokens, components, and a local interface kit. It does not replace the production planner. Read [source/provenance.md](source/provenance.md) for lineage and limits.

The owner-defined audiences are busy singles protecting their wardrobe, teens/newly independent students learning care, and a single mother managing a household of five. [AUDIENCE.md](AUDIENCE.md) records the specific needs, evidence limits and inclusive service principles. Quality of care must never depend on wealth or laundry experience.

## Visual foundations

Warm paper in light mode and deep navy in dark mode. Electric blue is the only interaction accent family. Information uses a quiet neutral hierarchy and compact grouped cards. Rounded controls, restrained soft elevation, and typographic identity carry the brand. The expressive surface is contained welcome artwork: the approved warm sign-in basket now leads first-use references; the original wardrobe still life remains preserved.

Use canonical declarations, never screenshot-sampled or guessed values. Original dictionaries and inspected production tokens remain separately recorded under source/.

## Spacing and layout

The bound base is **2px**, with observed optical increments 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 24, and 32px. The bound signature card padding is **14px 18px**. Final grouped source rows use **12px 16px** and a 12px tile-to-facts gap. These roles coexist; do not rewrite every row to the generic card inset.

Page gutter 16px, sheet body 24px. Tight fact stacks use 2–6px, groups 12–16px, sections 24–32px. Mobile is a natural-width scroll surface, never a fixed phone frame that clips real content. Default production shell width is at most 720px; focused reading tasks are at most 576px. Opt-in preparation tasks can use up to 72rem (1152px at default text size), with a two-column layout once their content container reaches 48rem: clothes and machine details on the left, settings, steps and the primary action on the right. Use at most two columns and keep the summary between 18rem and 22rem. Below that threshold, preserve the same DOM and reading order in one column; never remount controls on resize. Long or enlarged text must wrap without horizontal overflow.

Five root destinations retain the dock. Task flows replace it with a fixed or sticky glass footer. Sheets become centered modals at 720px and above; narrow screens use bottom sheets. Width does not determine touch capability: wide touch devices retain the grab handle and guarded swipe dismissal; only wide fine-pointer devices without a coarse pointer disable swiping. Footer and body respect safe areas and viewport height. Production measures fixed-footer height plus bottom offset and 16px clearance; reserve that actual space and keep focused controls visible without scrollIntoView. The dryer plan's handoff stays in normal document flow. The live preparation action is fixed on narrow screens and joins the settings column on wide screens; remove fixed-footer clearance when it becomes inline. Reflow must preserve entered minutes, focused controls, drawer scroll and unsaved choices. The rem-based threshold grows with enlarged root text; a tablet can remain single-column at double text size. Hinge avoidance remains separate from this viewport-resize acceptance. See [preview/spacing-tokens.html](preview/spacing-tokens.html).

The owner's wide-layout direction is demonstrated in [preview/wide-laundry-task.html](preview/wide-laundry-task.html). Use two purposeful task columns rather than stretching a vertical list. [source/foldable-layout.md](source/foldable-layout.md) records current React/Tailwind evidence, proposed wide-screen anatomy, segment-aware integration and the pending fold/unfold acceptance matrix. Ordinary responsive reflow does not establish physical hinge avoidance.

## Components

[COMPONENTS.md](COMPONENTS.md) defines a renderer-independent contract for the first four component families and their SwiftUI, Kotlin/Compose, web and Figma adaptations. [preview/component-contract.html](preview/component-contract.html) reuses the current browser kit for interactive review. Generated DTCG Light/Dark exchange files derive from canonical CSS; they do not replace it, install native fonts, synchronize design tools or establish runtime parity. Web styling-tool selection remains independent of the visual contract.

Component anatomy and the maintained implementation index live in [COMPONENTS.md](COMPONENTS.md). Shared shapes belong in components.css; app layout belongs in app.css; dryer-specific anatomy belongs in dryer-handoff.css. Preview-only composition may be local, but must not redefine a shared component's state or visual role.

Recognizable categories use the `choice-tile` family: art above a short visible label, a labeled group, a check badge plus ring when selected, and a column floor that drops columns at enlarged text. Only clothing type (4 columns) and product form (3 × 2) use tiles; short answers stay chips and sentence answers stay radio cards. Picked chips, answer chips and care-symbol tiles share one selected treatment with tiles: accent fill, selected ring and a check mark, never color alone. Secondary actions under a task instruction use `grouped-secondary-actions`: an explanation link named by what it explains (“Why here?” / “Why now?”), a “Step options” disclosure for departures, and a labeled “Something wrong?” recovery group of full-width outline rows. Each group is omitted when empty; the step keeps one filled primary. Review both in [preview/components-buttons.html](preview/components-buttons.html).

Short answers and action lists come in four shapes, chosen by how many there are and what they are (`AnswerRow`, `SegmentChoice` and `ActionList` in the application's laundry-ui). One shape per question; never a column of separate full-width outline buttons.

- **One row.** Two or three short answers to one question sit in one row of equal-width buttons, 44px at least (48px in a task footer), under the visible question: “Gone · Lighter · Still there”, “Put them together · Keep apart”, “Hang them · Wait for one”. The row wraps onto a second row when the text is large, never into a stack of full-width bars. The chosen answer is filled (`aria-pressed`); a plain action is outline; at most one is the filled primary.
- **Segment.** One choice out of a few that is kept as a setting (loads a day, how many loads the rack holds, “Washers free”, “Dryers free”, “Dry · Hang it · Not yet”) is the shared segment control. It wraps under large text and answers or saves on tap.
- **Grouped list.** Three or more actions, answers that are phrases, and any menu sit in one grouped card of divider rows: the recommended action first, rare or final actions (“Retire it”, “Remove”) last and quiet, an optional hint line under a row's title, and a caret at the end of a row that opens something. “What next?” and the Care tab's “More about this care” are grouped lists.
- **Side by side.** Only the two actions of one decision sit side by side, in the one-row shape. A primary never sits beside a list.

Compact actions use content-sized width while their text sibling receives spare space. Button heights are minimums; labels and facts may wrap. Full-width actions are deliberate task-footer or narrow-layout roles, not the default result of grid stretching. Equivalent queued loading actions use the same outline treatment and at least 48px targets.

No text is underlined. An action is a button or a row; a link (the explanation trigger, a source link, “{n} in the hamper”, a link inside help text) is semibold interactive-blue text with a 44px target and no underline, as in a native app. Hover and press change only its background.

## Interaction and domain invariants

- Plan status is derived from records. Unknown is a real answer, never inferred approval.
- An issue belongs to its garment or product. No separate global warning list.
- Open a sheet on a draft. Cancel/Escape/Close/backdrop/swipe leave records unchanged; if the draft differs from its opening values, ask “Discard your changes?” with Keep editing and Discard changes. Reverting the draft makes dismissal clean. Save writes a history entry, recalculates, and politely announces the change. A correction requires “I’ve checked.” If no choice is made after five seconds, return to Keep editing, preserve the draft and restore focus to the step heading. Explicit choices cancel the timer; a pending save never starts it. Only an explicit Discard removes the draft.
- Multi-step tasks keep one draft. Back keeps every answer. A fact row on a record opens its editor at that section or field; Save and Cancel return to the same record tab. For an existing record the drawer stays open after Save. New appliance and product intake keeps an Added panel open with Done and optional Add details; other creation flows retain their own accepted completion panels. While a save is pending, controls freeze. An error keeps the draft, points at the field and offers an inline retry. A success message stays visible when the parent re-renders. Action outcomes follow one convention: a success is a brief toast; a failed action stays as an inline `role="alert"` banner next to the section that acted, with “Try again” unless repeating could duplicate a record (adding an appliance offers none), until the next attempt or edit clears it. Never show an auto-dismissing error toast. Drafts live in the open page; never claim they survive a reload.
- An unsaved dryness check is kept on Back, and the plan's run row says “Unsaved check”. Only an explicit “Discard check”, confirmed with Keep editing / Discard, throws it away.
- Short help sits beside the question it explains. Supporting sources stay collapsed. A blocker stays visible, names the missing fact and offers the direct correction; finishing that correction returns to the plan. “Not sure” is an explicit postponement, never “No”. Sequence copy is honest: “One question first” only when there is one, otherwise “{n} questions first” and “Question n of N”.
- Wash/bleach/product restrictions cannot be overridden. Leaving a garment out changes only that load.
- Plan anytime, confirm at the actual machine, start only after confirmation and resolved requirements. Any source save invalidates confirmation.
- Shared machine labels never imply availability or reservation. Capacity can remain unknown; state “fit not checked.”
- Laundry-day estimates are checks, not promises. A planned time says “about”, a predicted or planned clock time or wait is rounded to 5 minutes, and a minute figure names its source; recorded times and times the person enters show as they are. A shared room shows no planned or predicted times. The app never calls a machine free: not on its own guess and not in its own words (“Nothing left in the dryer.”, never “The dryer is free.”). Held starts exist only at home. Dryer sharing is never preselected. See Laundry days.
- Product names never establish chemistry. Amounts come from the recorded pack table, never cap lines or defaults. A dose step draws the cap or scoop only from the product's recorded printed marks, filled to the planned mark; a mark that is not printed on the cap fills nothing, and no level is ever guessed.
- Drying is independent of the load's run sheet (“What goes in”). Four methods: Tumble dryer, Heated hanging dryer, Rack or line, Flat drying.
- Tumble evidence can be recorded. Natural drying is not recorded in this source. Rack/line and flat are “Not specified by the recorded label”; heated hanging is “Compatibility not established by recorded instructions.”
- A drying choice never edits the care label or declares a method safe. “This time only” ends at put-away; “Remember for this garment” is revocable with “Follow label again.”
- Real machine restrictions, dosing selection, capacity fit, conflict confirmation for other care labels, and concurrent timers remain production-planner responsibilities. The applied kit demonstrates a bounded source scenario only.

## Motion and accessibility

Press: 140ms and scale .98. Page navigation is immediate: no outgoing/incoming crossfade, entrance fade or browser snapshot, including account pages. Commit the new route when it is ready without waiting for photos to decode or an artificial animation delay. Show actual loading states when data is pending; do not delay the next action for motion. Current bottom drawer opens in 220ms with cubic-bezier(.32,0,.2,1), closes in 180ms with production ease-out cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1), and supports downward touch dismissal from its header. Generic 240ms sheet and Journey 120/200/280ms values remain historical roles; they do not drive current drawer motion. Preserve list identity and page scroll while a sheet draft is open. Center the close glyph in its 44px header control.

Reduced motion removes slides, scaling, and animation. No recurring idle loop is introduced. The source’s idle float/breathing animations conflict with its “no loops” handoff note; this package follows the restrained note and reduced-motion behavior.

Focus: 3px source-blue ring with offset. Sheet focus moves to the title and returns to the opener. Native dialog supplies modal focus trapping. Escape requests dismissal without saving, including the changed-draft confirmation. Every meaningful control is keyboard-reachable and at least 44px. Targets are 44pt iOS / 48dp Android when translated to native layouts.

Body and status text ≥4.5:1; large text/icons ≥3:1. Hover is limited to fine pointers; mobile actions use visible press feedback and never depend on hover. Hover keeps the foreground and changes only the paired background or border. Source-selected states must remain readable in both themes.

## Voice and copy

Calm, direct, specific. Explain the recorded fact, its implication, then the available action. Prefer “Wash instruction not recorded” over “Something went wrong.” “Unknown” and “Not recorded” are honest states, not user mistakes.

The load's run sheet is “What goes in” (“See what goes in”, “Back to what goes in”). “Chemistry” names a design-review page and a record family; it is never user-facing copy. Product records ask only for what the label prints: “Only record ingredients and directions printed on the label. Unknown ingredients need review.”, a “Treatment type on the label” field, and “Check this product’s label. A name or barcode doesn’t tell you what’s in it.”

Use “Allowed by the recorded label,” “Not specified by the recorded label,” and “Compatibility not established by recorded instructions” precisely. Never call an unspecified drying method recommended, compatible, or safe. Do not turn coarse “Needed by” into a precise finish-time promise. Laundry-day times say “about” (“done about 3:40 PM”, “We’ll remind you in about 25 min”), never “done at” a planned time, and no wait is called safe. Doses, capacities, weights, and account data in the source are illustrative, not real operational evidence.

## Anti-patterns

Do not introduce extra accent families, decorative gradients, additional font families, speculative safety/dose claims, available-machine badges, or looped celebrations. Do not flatten red conflict into yellow to-do. Do not detach issues from source rows. Do not create an override for wash/bleach restrictions. Do not promote historical Journey rules or retired drawings over the final source. Do not add shadows to every control, use a four-pixel-only grid, crop meaningful content images, duplicate a primary action in one viewport, or hide long names behind truncation.

OpenDesign owns the design system and screen mockups. Current tokens, previews, and screens are editable in place. Historical source_examples/ artifacts explain the original decisions; they do not impose handoff restrictions on the current workspace.
## Laundry patterns

### Home, records and first use

- Home summarizes bounded care counts and offers an actionable care-details row. Keep the full garment list in Closet.
- Garment records use Care / Details / History, with an honest empty history. Refresh belongs under Care. Matching-piece counts link to the hamper; add/remove are direct scoped actions.

### Laundry days

The October 6 laundry-day screens plan a day of washes around one washer and one dryer at home, and keep a shared room to order and counts. The app leads; these rules describe it. Copy is verbatim from the app; example names, times and counts are illustrative.

- **Estimates.** Every planned time is an estimate. It says “about”, any predicted or planned clock time or wait is rounded to 5 minutes, and a minute figure names where it came from: “(from your last 3 washes)”, nothing extra for a recorded program time; in the day's order “your washer’s time”, “your last 3 washes”, “your dryer’s time”. Minutes come only from a recorded program time or the person's own last washes; the app never asks for a guess up front and has no machine-times question. Recorded times and times the person enters are shown as they are, not rounded: “washed 9:01”, “started 9:02 PM”, and “2 loads fit before 10:41 PM.” for a “Home until?” of 10:41 PM. With wash or dryer minutes missing there is no clock: order and counts still show, with “We’ll show times after the first wash and dry.”
- **No clock in a shared room.** A shared room or laundromat shows order and counts only: no end time, no “Home until?”, no “Next” card, no held start and no timed reminder. The app cannot know when someone else's machine frees up. Recorded times and the person's own wash timer still show (“Washer 1 · started 9:00 PM”, “Time is up”): only planned or predicted times are left out.
- **Never call a machine free.** At home only the household's own tracked runs make the dryer busy; an untimed run reads “Is the dryer free?”. In a shared room the app asks. At the start of a pass the footer asks “What’s free right now?”, with a quiet “Skip” link and two labelled segment rows, “Washers free” and “Dryers free” (1 · 2 · 3+). The answers size only the first starts (“Start 1 wash now. One for each free dryer, plus loads that hang to dry.”, the footer link “Start all 3 now” with “Some may have to wait for a dryer, or hang them.”, later “Start the rest once a wash is done.”), mark planned loads “Start now” or “Later”, stay on the phone and are never machine status. The number is still asked at each machine. There is no exception: when the early check takes every piece out, its result ends “Nothing left in the dryer.”, which reports what the person emptied and says nothing about the machine being free.
- **One order, one numbering.** Loads are offered urgent first (damp, sweaty, smelly, baby mess, a fresh stain, or needed by tomorrow), then tumble loads with two-run loads first, then air-dry loads last. “Load n of N” follows that order on Home, Laundry, the day strip and the load pages. Copy never claims the best order.
- **The “Next” card (home with one dryer).** One card on Laundry, labelled “Next”, with the next wash timed against the dryer. When the next wash would wait no longer than the wait limit for the dryer, the steps join: “Move them to the dryer, then start load 2” (or “Start load 2 now”) with “Load 2: Towels · about 50 min · it may wait about 15 min for the dryer”. When it would wait longer, the start is held: “Move them to the dryer” with “Then start load 2 at about 4:10 PM, so it’s done when the dryer is.”, or “Next at about 4:10 PM: start load 2” with “Load 2: Towels · about 50 min · done when the dryer is, at about 5:30 PM. Started now, it would wait about 40 min for the dryer.” and “We’ll remind you at about 4:10 PM.”. The card opens with a small “Next” label. While the home dryer is running it adds a “Dryer says” field (min) with the hint “Type the minutes left on the dryer to re-time this.” above a full-width tonal “Save”. Held starts and their reminders exist only at home.

### Household water

- Household → Appliances has a Water section below the appliance groups and the Add row: one row with a drop icon. Its title is the band (“Soft”, “Moderately hard”, “Hard”, “Very hard”, “Not known”) or “Not set”; its fact is where the band came from, or “How hard your tap water is” when nothing is set. The row opens the Water sheet.
- The first answer is a ZIP code, one step at a time: “What’s your ZIP code?” (five digits, numeric keyboard, hint “Only the ZIP code is saved, never your location.”, error “A ZIP code is five digits.”) → “Which water bill do you pay?” (EBMUD (East Bay MUD) / Not listed / Well or other) → for EBMUD only, “Which town is your home in?” (“Pick your town”; hint “Some towns can get harder water part of the year. Pick “Not sure” if you don’t know.”) → Save. EBMUD is offered only for a ZIP where it can be the bill and preselected only where it is the one bill; otherwise the step first says “We don’t have your water company yet. A strip reading still works.” and offers only Not listed and Well or other. Never ask for GPS, device location or an address; only the typed ZIP code is saved.
- The result is a facts card: Water (the band), From (the source line) and ZIP code (opens the ZIP step). Source lines: “EBMUD, {town}”, “Water company not listed”, “Well or other”, “Strip reading, {date}”, “Your water report, {date}”, “Softener and strip, {date}”, “Softener, no strip reading”, “You said you don’t know”.
- Bands follow USGS hardness as CaCO3: up to 60 mg/L soft, 61–120 moderately hard, 121–180 hard, over 180 very hard (1 grain per gallon = 17 mg/L). A town takes the highest figure of every treatment plant that can serve it (“Not sure” takes them all); a plant without a figure keeps an otherwise soft town Not known. A well, an unlisted bill, an unmatched ZIP, or a lookup whose utility report is older than last year’s edition is Not known, never a guess. A household’s own reading keeps its band however old its report is: an old hard report still reads Hard or Very hard, and only an old soft one reads “Soft, not counted” (below).
- Under the card, “Did you test it yourself?” offers outline rows: “Enter a strip reading”, “I have a softener”, “Enter a number from my water report”, “I don’t know”, “Change ZIP code”. A reading step (“Strip reading”, “Softener”, “Water report”) asks Reading and Unit (“grains per gallon (gpg)”, “mg/L or ppm”), plus “Year of the report” for a report, with its hint (“If the strip shows a range, enter the higher number.”; “A softener counts only with a strip reading from a tap it feeds. Leave this empty if you haven’t tested.”; “Use the highest hardness number your report lists.”) and errors “Enter the number from the strip or report.” / “Enter the year printed on the report.” An override always wins over the lookup; a softener counts only through its strip reading. Changing the ZIP starts over: the town and any override belonged to the old address.
- A soft reading that no longer counts reads “Soft, not counted” with “Your water can change with the season. Test again.” or, for an old report, “This report is too old to count. Enter a newer one or test again.” Where a harder plant can serve the town (or the town is unknown), a strip keeps soft for 30 days; a softener’s strip keeps it for 6 months; a report’s number counts only from last year’s edition or newer and, on an EBMUD bill, only where no harder plant can serve the town.
- The band changes no dose yet; every dose is still the label’s amount. Production copy never implies otherwise. Only the trial build adds, under the row and on the result, the trial-only note “Your water doesn’t change the dose yet. Every dose is still the label’s amount.”

Review the row, the three steps and the result in [preview/production-patterns.html](preview/production-patterns.html) (Household water).

### Chemistry records and treatment

- Exact-product lipase is explicitly listed, confirmed absent, or not established. Generic “enzymes,” a product name or an omitted ingredient establishes neither presence nor absence. Capture exact variant, market/pack identity and source; identity or recorded-ingredient changes require another check.
- Recurring odor/oily/stiff/waxy observations capture when they appear and the previous wash/additive/machine context. They do not diagnose, choose chemicals or increase dose.
- Ingredient-role explanations are optional disclosures with 44px targets, keyboard activation and visible focus. Restrictions and product directions remain visible in their own records.
- Pretreatment requires explicit selection and garment/stain inspection. Selection is not physical performance. Recorded-step recovery requires explicit retry; production replay is household-scoped.
- Container soaking requires an explicitly selected, supported treatment. Record the label's water basis, measured dose unit, minimum/maximum time, temperature limit, permitted materials and transfer directions. Ask for actual measured water and temperature, then snapshot the scaled dose and start time. Drum volume and garment counts are not water measurements. General wool/silk soaking is excluded. Before ready, completion is unavailable; overdue treatment requires label-directed removal and reassessment, never a stronger automatic recipe.
- Garment History attaches improved/unchanged/worse/irritation/damage and assessment stage to a specific finished wash. Preserve actual product/dose records and the original observation. Reassessment points to instructions, wash/rinse, machine, drying and storage; it does not infer effectiveness or diagnose irritation.
